# Results

## Depth-matched long-read data retain distinct read and alignment profiles

To establish a common input baseline, we generated nested 10×, 30× and 50× subsets from HG002 BGI, ONT and PacBio HiFi reads and carried the same subsets through the alignment workflows. Approximate input depth differed from the target by at most 0.011× at 10× and 30× (Fig. 1a). At the nominal 50× tier, BGI and HiFi reached 49.978× and 49.993×; the ONT subset reached 47.768× from the available reads. This design provided matched 10× and 30× comparisons and an explicitly measured high-depth tier.

At the matched 30× tier, the platforms produced distinct read-length profiles (Fig. 1b). ONT showed the highest values across all three length summaries, with a mean length of 18.42 kb, a median of 17.36 kb and an N50 of 29.40 kb. BGI reads had a mean length of 16.19 kb, a median of 12.56 kb and an N50 of 25.95 kb. HiFi reads had a mean length of 14.02 kb, a median of 14.60 kb and an N50 of 16.77 kb.

Reported read Q-score composition separated the same datasets along a second axis (Fig. 1b). HiFi reads had a mean Q score of 25.8 and a median of 30.9, and 93.2% of reads had mean Q > 20. The corresponding values were 16.5, 25.5 and 78.3% for ONT, and 16.8, 19.2 and 33.6% for BGI. Reads passing this mean-Q threshold contributed 88.53 Gb for HiFi, 80.13 Gb for ONT and 38.92 Gb for BGI, equivalent to 91.7%, 83.0% and 40.3% of the respective 30× yields. At equal total yield, the three platforms provided distinct combinations of read length and reported read Q-score composition.

To compare reference representation after alignment, we measured the fraction of reference bases covered at ≥1× across three depths, two reference genomes and two aligners, giving 36 platform–depth–reference–aligner conditions (Fig. 1c). Coverage breadth increased in every condition, with gains of 0.51–0.83 percentage points from 10× to 30× and 0.12–0.22 percentage points from 30× to 50×; the corresponding median gains were 0.65 and 0.185 percentage points. At 30×, GRCh38 coverage breadth spanned 92.94–93.33% across the six platform–aligner combinations, a range of 0.39 percentage points. T2T-CHM13 coverage breadth spanned 98.31–99.43%, and the platform–aligner range widened to 1.12 percentage points: BGI and ONT reached 99.33–99.43%, whereas HiFi reached 98.31–98.38%. For matched platform and aligner settings, the 30× breadth was 5.26–6.31 percentage points higher on T2T-CHM13 than on GRCh38.

The two aligners gave closely matched coverage breadth values across the full matrix (Fig. 1c). The median signed difference between winnowmap and minimap2 was −0.025 percentage points, the median absolute difference was 0.050 percentage points and the maximum absolute difference was 0.21 percentage points. The resulting coverage matrix showed diminishing gains beyond 30×, near platform convergence on GRCh38 and wider platform separation on T2T-CHM13.

Read-level retention remained distinct at 30× (Fig. 1d). Primary mapped-read rates ranged from 99.02% to 99.69% for BGI, 97.94% to 98.34% for ONT and 99.92% to 100.00% for HiFi across the four reference–aligner settings. Every setting retained the same platform order, HiFi followed by BGI and ONT, with a between-platform spread of 1.66–1.99 percentage points. Within matched platform–reference pairs, switching from minimap2 to winnowmap changed the primary mapped-read rate by −0.36 to +0.01 percentage points. The paired position-level and read-level measurements showed that similar coverage breadth could coexist with distinct proportions of reads retained as primary alignments.

The same 30× matrix also differed in BAM output footprint (Extended Data Fig. 1). BAM sizes ranged from 90.51 to 109.30 GiB for BGI, 81.35 to 102.88 GiB for ONT and 34.45 to 41.01 GiB for HiFi. Under matched reference and aligner settings, the BGI and ONT files were 2.36–2.72 times the size of the corresponding HiFi files. Winnowmap outputs were 6.6–11.5% smaller than minimap2 outputs across all six platform–reference comparisons, and T2T-CHM13 outputs were 7.0–15.3% smaller than GRCh38 outputs across all six platform–aligner comparisons. These values quantify storage requirements under the current alignment and output settings.

To examine residual error composition after alignment, we quantified apparent mismatch, insertion and deletion bases in 30× GRCh38 pilot windows for each platform and aligner (Extended Data Fig. 2). The analysis covered 2,981,290 region bases, masked 6,488 GIAB truth sites, retained primary alignments with mapping quality ≥20 and counted bases with quality ≥20. Across the two aligners, total apparent-error burdens were 17.239–17.552 per 1,000 aligned Q20 bases for BGI, 6.351–6.436 for ONT and 1.101–1.117 for HiFi. Deletion bases accounted for 85.4–85.7% of the BGI burden, 84.5–84.6% of the ONT burden and 57.3–58.6% of the HiFi burden. Relative to minimap2, winnowmap changed the total burden by −1.78% for BGI, −1.32% for ONT and +1.47% for HiFi, preserving the platform order and the main component structure.

Together, depth matching controlled sequencing dosage, and platform-specific structure remained visible in read profiles, reference-dependent coverage breadth, primary mapping retention, BAM footprint and apparent alignment residuals. These measurements define the input context for interpreting downstream variant calling, phasing and assembly analyses. Reported read Q scores and the masked-window residuals characterize the present data and alignment workflow; donor-specific analyses are required to estimate intrinsic sequencing error.
